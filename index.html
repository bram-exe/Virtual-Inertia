Virtual Inertia Market Design

Bram Copeland · Jasmine Cheng · Andrew Weskamp

In collaboration with Grid United

Summary & Abstract

We are developing a market mechanism for buying and selling virtual inertia on the power grid. Inertia is keeps the grid frequency stable at 60 Hz, and is created by rotating masses. The purpose is to visualize imbursement for inertia supplying resources for their contributions to grid stability. The design will include an in depth report, as well as a physical representation of the model design. The report will include pricing, communication, and other aspects that dictate an ancillary market. We are working with Grid United to develop the mechanism, with our home base in the CSU Powerhouse campus.

Project Background

Conventional energy sources like natural gas, coal, and hydropower use heavy, rotating turbine generators that spin synchronously with the power grid's frequency of 60Hz. The rotating mass of these generators creates an inertia that helps maintain grid frequency when generation and load fall out of balance. When an imbalance happens, it’s initially absorbed by the kinetic energy stored in the spinning masses, which slows the rate of change of frequency (ROCOF) and stops the frequency from changing too much. Inverter based resources (IBRs), such as wind, solar, and battery storage lack inertia for grid stability support. The push towards the more sustainable IBR sources means that grids are more susceptible to a larger ROCOF when generation and load are out of balance. Inertia is currently not a service anyone chooses to provide, there’s no wholesale market product that compensates a resource for providing it. In our project, we aim to design a market mechanism for buying and selling virtual inertia on the power grid.

Goals & Constraints

Goals

The first objective of this project is to propose an engineering solution for a virtual inertia market. The proposal will be in the form of a report that will be submitted to both the our industry partner Grid United and CSU's senior design class for Engineering Days. Our second objective is to create a simulation to go along with the report, where we will demonstrate the market mechanism for Grid United and E-Days. The scope of the project includes market pricing and selling rates for inertia generating resources, as well as market design. The design and distribution of inertia is out of our defined scope and will not be included in the project deliverables.

Constraints

We anticipate running into constraints such as access to data of current grid information, as the United States grid is secure knowledge. Our timeline may also be a limiting factor, as we are a new project and must build the foundation first. Specifically, we will define what the model must include to meet our goals before scaling it beyond our capabilities.
